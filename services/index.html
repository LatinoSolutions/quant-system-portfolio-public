How an engagement works

Three steps, each with a clear end.

  1. Step 1

    Diagnostic

    Fixed scope

    A short, read-only look at how your team works with AI agents today, and a concrete recommendation for your case. Low risk: you decide afterwards whether to go further.

    • How each person uses agents today
    • Risks: secrets, permissions, missing tests, who reviews what
    • Which tasks an agent can do alone, with review, or never
    • A workspace design for your repository and tools
    • A plan for the first month
    • You get: a written report and a readout call

    Limits. Read-only access to one repository. Your code is not changed during the diagnostic.

  2. Step 2

    Custom implementation

    On your tools

    The workflow, built into your own GitHub or GitLab, CI and agent tools, then run end to end on real tasks with your team.

    • Task isolation: one workspace and branch per task
    • Agent policies: instructions, allowed files, no-go zones
    • Test gates: nothing merges if the suite gets worse than main
    • Independent review on the routes you agree
    • Human approval points where they matter, such as payments or customer data
    • Metrics, logs and alerts: first-pass approval, rework, review rounds, tokens where the provider reports them
    • Runbook and handover, so your team runs it without me

    Limits. Agents get no production access. A test gate protects only what your tests cover; I add minimal smoke tests where the gate needs them, not a full test suite.

  3. Step 3

    Maintenance and improvements

    Optional

    Updates, tuning from your own metrics, new integrations and fixes, in the format that suits you.

    Hour bank

    A block of hours agreed in advance, used as you need it and reported against the work done.

    Per intervention

    One request at a time, scoped and quoted before any work starts.

    Clear lines. Defects in what I delivered are fixed under the warranty we agree, not billed as maintenance. New scope is quoted separately. Support runs on business days; there is no 24/7 on-call.

Case study · proprietary platform

AWOP · Agentic Workspace Orchestration Platform

Proprietary platform · built end to end · since September 2026

Multi-agent orchestration (several AI agents working at once) with MCP (a standard connector that lets an outside AI assistant operate it), evals (every agent run measured by model and role) and model routing with failover (each job goes to a chosen model, and moves to another account when one runs out of quota).

Several AI agents work in parallel under one shared engineering standard. It is where the method I offer was built and where it is applied: the trading decision engine and the execution copilot are developed with it, and the energy research platform was.

Its limits, stated up front: agents work in isolated branches and working copies, which is not a security sandbox; and independent review is mandatory on autopilot merges (changes that merge without a person, on routes agreed in advance), not on every merge.
