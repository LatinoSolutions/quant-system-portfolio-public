Case study · Intraday Forecasting & Trading Co-pilot

The trader sets the limits.
The co-pilot forecasts the day and executes inside them.

Bounded autonomy for intraday trading. The co-pilot forecasts how the trading day is unfolding, proposes the trade and sends it to MetaTrader 5, either with the trader's confirmation or through an autopilot that can only act inside limits the trader arms.

The problem

Intraday, a trader has to read how the day is unfolding and act fast. One wrong size or price in a manual order can cost real money, and a fully automatic system that nobody can switch off is a risk no trading desk accepts.

What it does

An intraday forecasting engine tracks the state of the trading day, updates the next price targets and classifies how the day is likely to end. It is recalibrated every night from 6,726 historical sessions. A macro news outlook frames swing decisions. When a trade fits, the co-pilot proposes it, and the order reaches MetaTrader 5 in one of two ways:

  1. Trader confirmation. The trader reviews the proposal and confirms it.
  2. Bounded autopilot. The trader arms a permission per symbol: risk, reward-to-risk, a stop time and a maximum number of attempts. The autopilot only sends orders inside that permission, and switches off at the stop time.

A separate execution layer validates, sizes and publishes every order, whichever path it came from.

Who sent each order

Built for failure

  • Hash-chained journal. Every event is recorded with the hash of the one before it: 54,763 records so far, so the history cannot change silently.
  • No blind retries. When a broker submission is uncertain, it goes to reconciliation against the broker's state instead of being sent again.
  • Autonomy with an off switch. The autopilot only runs while the trader's permission is armed, and stops at the trader's stop time.
  • Checked position controls. Close all, close and break-even are validated against a fresh read of the trading terminal and logged before anything is sent.

How it was measured

  • Snapshot taken on 16 Nov 2026 from the co-pilot's journal, read only.
  • An order counts once it is published to MetaTrader 5. Its origin, trader or autopilot, is read from the journal record.
  • The 6,726 sessions are the historical day records the forecasts are recalibrated from, across the instruments the co-pilot follows.

Stated limit. A published order is an order sent, not a fill or a profit. The forecasting engine is in active research: this page claims no accuracy figure and no trading result.
